## 1. The position in one sentence

For an inverse problem whose target object is not directly accessible, it is often more productive — and more honest — to first prove as many *necessary conditions* on any admissible solution as possible (locating and bounding the gap), and only then to attempt a construction. This is a **research method**, not a result, and it is not new; it is the disciplined practice of much of mature mathematics and physics, stated explicitly so it can be applied deliberately.

## 2. What the method is

Given a target $T$ (an algebra, a mechanism, a structure) that cannot be exhibited directly, the method proceeds:

1. **State the success conditions precisely.** What would a solution have to satisfy to count as one? (For the G_SM gap: conditions G1–G4.)
2. **Localize the gap.** Identify the exact step at which no existing construction succeeds, rather than treating the whole problem as uniformly open. (For G_SM: the enlargement of the abelian single-mode algebra to a non-abelian one — multiplicity $\geq 2$.)
3. **Prove necessary conditions / lower bounds.** Establish what *any* admissible solution must contain, independent of whether one is exhibited. (For G_SM: multiplicity $\geq 2$, rank $\geq 4$, KO-dimension-6 real structure, a chirality mechanism.)
4. **Catalogue candidates against the bounds.** Test existing programmes against the necessary conditions; exclude those that fail a bound before any dynamical analysis.
5. **Only then construct** — now within a sharply constrained search space, with the failure modes already mapped.

The yield of steps 1–4 is real even if step 5 never succeeds: a clean no-go plus quantitative lower bounds is a publishable result, because it tells every future model-builder what a solution must minimally contain and where the difficulty actually lives.

## 3. Why it is honest as well as productive

The method has a built-in defence against the most common failure of ambitious theory: mistaking a desired construction for an achieved one. By proving necessary conditions *first*, one cannot accidentally "derive" the target by quietly inserting it — any insertion shows up as a condition that was assumed rather than forced. In the G_SM analysis this is exactly what surfaced: every candidate route reaches the target only by inserting the multiplicity/real-structure data that the gap analysis identifies as the missing piece. The method made the insertion visible instead of letting it hide inside a fluent construction.

## 4. The precedent (the method is not novel)

Mature mathematics routinely proves existence conditions, uniqueness conditions, no-go theorems, minimality conditions, and necessary symmetries *before* constructing an object. The Erlangen programme characterizes geometries by their invariance groups; Noether's theorem reads conservation laws off symmetries; obstruction theory asks what must vanish before a structure can exist; representation theory bounds what can act on a given space before any specific action is built. "Characterize the admissible solution space via necessary invariants, then construct" is the shared spine of these. The UIDT contribution is not the method but a worked, non-trivial instance of it (the G_SM gap) carried out with explicit evidence discipline.

## 5. The motivating analogy — and its strict limits

The method was partly motivated by an analogy with inverse problems in perception: one does not observe an underlying reality $R$ directly but only the response $F_\alpha(R)$ of a perceptual operator, and across a family of such operators $\{F_\alpha\}$ one may ask which features are *invariant*. The analogy is a **motivation source only**, and three limits are binding:

1. **It is [E] and stays [E].** The perception analogy motivates the *shape* of the method (seek invariants / necessary conditions rather than the object). It provides no evidence for anything.
2. **An invariant over $\{F_\alpha\}$ is a property of the family, not of $R$.** Even if one proved which features survive all perceptual transformations, whether those features belong to an observer-independent reality or merely to the shared structure of the operators is **not decidable within the formalism**. The analogy therefore cannot export a reality claim.
3. **No bridge to consciousness or to $E_T$.** This document makes no connection between the method and any neurodynamic, altered-state, or torsion-energy quantity. Such connections remain [E] and require a validated scale bridge that does not exist; they are out of scope here and must not be inferred.

## 6. What may and may not be claimed

**May be claimed:**
- "Gap Localization before Construction" is a disciplined, productive method for inverse problems with inaccessible targets.
- The G_SM gap analysis is a worked instance that produced real results (a localized obstruction and quantitative lower bounds on the admissible algebra).
- The method has a built-in anti-self-deception property (insertions become visible).

**May NOT be claimed:**
- That the method, or its perception analogy, brings UIDT "closer to reality" in any ontological sense.
- That invariance over perceptual operators reveals properties of an observer-independent reality.
- That the method is a novel discovery rather than an explicit statement of established mathematical practice.
- Any link between the method, $E_T$, and consciousness or altered states.

## 7. The honest standing claim

> The cumulative UIDT gap-analysis demonstrates a methodological stance — characterize the admissible solution space via necessary conditions before attempting construction — that is productive and self-disciplining on at least one non-trivial example. Whether this stance constitutes a transferable meta-scientific contribution is itself an open question that would require several independent worked examples, each producing new and testable results, before it could be asserted. Until then it is recorded as a working method, at [E], separate from every physical claim.
