## Executive Summary

The canonical non-minimal coupling κ = 0.500 ± 0.008 [A] requires a UV explanation:
either a symmetry that protects κ from radiative corrections, or a matching relation
that generates κ ~ O(1) without trans-Planckian inputs. This note presents three
mechanisms, each with explicit matching formulae and O(1)-parameter benchmarks.

**[TENSION ALERT]** All three mechanisms remain [D] (unverified predictions)
until independent amplitude calculation and/or lattice confirmation.

## Mechanism 1 — Discrete Shift Symmetry (Preferred) [D]

### Symmetry Principle

Impose on the scalar sector:
$$S \to S + \frac{2\pi}{N} \quad (\mathbb{Z}_N \text{ shift symmetry})$$

This symmetry forbids a mass term $m_S^2 S^2$ and forces the scalar potential to be
of the cosine type $V(S) = v_0^4[1 - \cos(S/f)]$, which for small fluctuations around
the minimum reduces to the quartic form with:
$$\lambda_S = \frac{v_0^4}{2f^4}$$

The non-minimal coupling generated by anomaly matching under $\mathbb{Z}_N$:
$$\kappa = \frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi} \cdot C(r) \cdot N_{\text{species}}$$

where $C(r)$ is the Dynkin index of the representation under which the anomaly contributors
transform and $N_{\text{species}}$ counts the number of species.

### Matching Condition for κ = 0.500

$$\kappa = \frac{g_3^2}{16\pi^2} \cdot T_R \cdot N_{\text{species}}$$

With $g_3^2 \approx 4\pi \times 0.35 \approx 4.40$ (at $\mu \sim m_S$ [D]) and $T_R = 1/2$
(fundamental representation):

$$N_{\text{species}} = \frac{16\pi^2 \cdot \kappa}{g_3^2 \cdot T_R}
= \frac{16\pi^2 \times 0.5}{4.40 \times 0.5} \approx 35.5$$

**Benchmark:** $N_{\text{species}} \approx 36$ species in fundamental SU(3) representation.
This is O(10) and physically plausible in a composite or extra-dimensional model [D].

### Naturalness Assessment

Under $\mathbb{Z}_N$ symmetry, κ is an anomaly coefficient and therefore:
- Protected against polynomial UV corrections: $\delta\kappa_{\rm UV} = 0$ to all orders
- Only radiative corrections from explicit symmetry-breaking terms enter
- Fine-tuning metric: $\Delta_{\rm FT} = 1$ (technically natural) [D]

**Acceptance criterion:** Identify $N_{\text{species}}$ field content in a concrete model
and verify $T_R \cdot N_{\text{species}} \approx 17.75$ holds from spectrum [D].

## Mechanism 2 — Large-N Collective Matching [D]

### Construction

In a theory with $N_f$ heavy Dirac fermions $\Psi_i$ ($i = 1,\ldots,N_f$) with
Yukawa coupling $y$ to the scalar $S$:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\rm UV} \supset y \sum_{i=1}^{N_f} S\bar{\Psi}_i\Psi_i + M_F \bar{\Psi}_i\Psi_i$$

Integrating out $\Psi_i$ at 1-loop generates:

$$\kappa_{\rm eff} = \frac{y^2 N_f T_F}{16\pi^2} \cdot \frac{\Lambda_{\rm UV}}{M_F}$$

### Matching Equation

$$y^2 = \frac{16\pi^2 \kappa M_F}{N_f T_F \Lambda_{\rm UV}}$$

### Benchmark Table

| $N_f$ | $M_F / \Lambda_{\rm UV}$ | $y_{\rm required}$ | Natural? |
|--------|--------------------------|---------------------|----------|
| 1 | 1.0 | 3.95 | Marginal (y < 4π) |
| 3 | 1.0 | 2.28 | Yes [D] |
| 10 | 1.0 | 1.25 | Yes [D] |
| 3 | 0.3 | 1.32 | Yes [D] |
| 10 | 0.3 | 0.72 | **Optimal** [D] |

**Optimal benchmark:** $N_f = 10$, $M_F = 0.3\,\Lambda_{\rm UV}$, $y \approx 0.72$ —
all parameters O(1), no trans-Planckian values [D].

### Naturalness

$$\Delta_{\rm FT}(y) = \left|\frac{\partial \ln \kappa}{\partial \ln y}\right| = 2$$

At 2-loop order, $\kappa$ receives corrections $\delta\kappa \sim y^4 N_f/(16\pi^2)^2$,
giving a relative correction of order $y^2 N_f / 16\pi^2 \sim 0.05$ for the optimal
benchmark — **technically natural** [D].

## Mechanism 3 — Sequestered Extra Dimension [D]

### Setup

In a Randall-Sundrum-type scenario with the visible brane at $z = L$ and a
hidden brane at $z = 0$ (sequestered sector):

$$\kappa_{\rm 4D} = \int_0^L dz\, e^{-4k|z|} \kappa_{\rm 5D}(z)$$

For a bulk scalar $S$ with bulk mass $m_5^2 = (4 - \epsilon)k^2$ (near BF bound):

$$\kappa_{\rm 4D} \approx \kappa_{\rm 5D} \cdot \left(\frac{1 - e^{-4kL}}{4k}\right) \cdot e^{-4kL}$$

### Matching for κ = 0.500

With $kL \approx 11.5$ (standard RS1 hierarchy), $\kappa_{\rm 5D}$ of O(1):

$$\kappa_{\rm 5D} = \frac{\kappa_{\rm 4D} \cdot 4k}{1 - e^{-4kL}} \approx 4k \cdot \kappa_{\rm 4D}$$

For $k = 1$ (in units of the 5D Planck mass) and $\kappa_{\rm 4D} = 0.5$:
$$\kappa_{\rm 5D} \approx 2.0$$

This is **O(1) in 5D Planck units** — fully natural [D].

### Naturalness

Sequestering suppresses bulk-to-brane transmission of radiative corrections.
κ receives no additive renormalisation from hidden-sector loops, ensuring
$\delta\kappa_{\rm hidden} \approx 0$ [D].
